Privacy Policy

Work Command · Work Command Notes · Effective 1 October 2026

Work Command is a personal notes and to-do app. It comes as a Mac app, and as the website Work Command Notes at jordanshipwreck.github.io/work-command-notes. This policy explains what the app does with your information. The short version: your notes live in your own Google Drive, the app has no server of its own, and nobody but you can read them.

Who we are

Work Command is built and run by Jordan Stapp, an individual developer. Contact: jordanshipwreck@gmail.com.

What the app accesses in your Google account

  • Only the files you choose, or files the app creates for you, in Google Drive. Work Command uses Google's drive.file permission. It cannot see the rest of your Drive. You pick your notes folder with Google's own file picker.
  • Your basic account identity (your email address and a Google account identifier), so the app can show which account is signed in and keep one person's choices separate from another's on the same device.

The app reads and writes the notes in that folder, which are plain text (Markdown) files, so you can see and edit your Days, to-dos and pages. That is all it does with them.

Where your information is kept

  • Your notes stay in your Google Drive. Work Command has no server of its own, and the website keeps no database on a server. The only other copies of your notes are the ones on your own devices, described below.
  • On the website, your browser keeps the sign-in for the current tab only (it ends when the tab closes), plus a few small preferences on your device: your theme, whether you hid the Brain's folder list, the account that is signed in, which Drive files you picked, the link suggestions you dismissed (the page and the name each one was about) and, if the Mac has set a Weather place, that place and the last forecast the website received for it. Nothing is sent to us.
  • On the website, a copy of your notes is kept on your device so the site keeps working with no connection. Your browser's own storage (IndexedDB) holds the Days and pages you have opened or that the site read ahead for you, and anything you wrote while offline until it reaches your Drive. It stays on that device, in that browser, and is not sent to us. Signing out or changing the Brain folder removes it. The site's service worker keeps only the site's own files (its pages, scripts, styles, fonts and icons), never your notes.
  • In the Mac app, the Google sign-in is kept in your Mac's Keychain. The Mac keeps a working copy of your notes folder on your own disk, so the app is fast and works offline, and it syncs changes with your Drive. The Mac app also keeps its own local database on your Mac, for its settings, an index of your Days and to-dos, and a record of what it has done for you. It stays on your Mac and is not sent to us.

Your calendar on the website (optional)

In the Mac app, Settings has a switch called Show my calendar on the website. It is off unless you turn it on. When it is on, the Mac writes a small file, Agenda.md, into the Work Command folder inside your notes folder. That file holds the titles, start and end times and calendar names of your meetings for today and the next 7 days, for the calendars you chose to show, and nothing else about them. The website reads that file to show your agenda. Like your other notes, it lives in your notes folder and may be part of the copy kept on your device. Turning the switch off removes the file from your notes folder when the Mac can reach that folder. If it cannot, the app tells you so, the file is left where it is, and the Mac tries again the next time it starts. Once the file is gone from your notes folder, its removal reaches your Drive and your other devices through the normal sync.

Weather (optional)

If you add the Weather widget and choose a place in the Mac app, the app asks Open-Meteo, a free weather service, for that place's forecast. The request carries the place's latitude and longitude, its time zone and your temperature unit. On the Mac the request is sent from your Mac; on the website it is sent from your browser. In the Mac app, while you search for a place, the words you type are sent to Open-Meteo too. Open-Meteo also sees what any website sees of a request, such as your IP address. Nothing about you, your account or your notes is sent. If you never choose a place, nothing is sent.

The website has no place picker of its own. The Mac keeps a copy of your Day layout (which widgets your Day shows and where) in a file called Day layout.md in the Work Command folder inside your notes folder, so the website can draw the same Day. If you have chosen a Weather place, that file also holds the place's name, latitude, longitude and time zone and your temperature unit, and the website reads them from it. Like your other notes, that file lives in your Drive and may be part of the copy kept on your device.

What we never do

  • We do not sell, rent or share your information with anyone.
  • We do not use your information for advertising.
  • We do not run analytics, tracking pixels or third-party trackers.
  • We do not use data from Google APIs to develop, improve or train any artificial intelligence or machine learning model.
  • No person reads your notes. The developer has no way to reach them.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Work Command's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Assistant features in the Mac app

The Mac app has optional assistant features, such as drafting a message or summarising a meeting. They work only if you add your own key for an AI provider (for example OpenAI) in the app's settings. When you use one of those features, the text that feature needs is sent from your Mac to the provider you chose, under your own account with them and their terms. That text does not go to us, and you can remove the key at any time. If you choose an AI that runs on your own Mac instead (such as Ollama), that text stays on your Mac. The website has no assistant features.

The Mac app can also let the Claude desktop app (or Codex) read your notes, if you turn that on in Settings › Integrations. It is off unless you turn it on, it only reads (it cannot change anything), and it works only while Work Command is open on your Mac. What Claude reads becomes part of your conversation with Claude, under your own Claude account and Anthropic's terms.

Services the app connects to

  • Google (sign-in, the file picker and Google Drive), under Google's Privacy Policy.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the website's files. GitHub may keep standard web server logs, such as your IP address, under GitHub's Privacy Statement.
  • Open-Meteo, only if you choose a place for the Weather widget (see above), under Open-Meteo's terms.
  • An AI provider you choose, only in the Mac app and only if you add your own key (see above).
  • Claude (Anthropic), only if you turn on the Claude connection in the Mac app (see above).

Your choices

  • Sign out at any time in Settings. On the website, that also clears these from your device: the account that is signed in, the Drive files you picked, your dismissed link suggestions, the Weather place and forecast, and the copy of your notes. Your theme and whether you hid the folder list stay, since they hold nothing about you.
  • Remove Work Command's access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Your notes remain yours. They are ordinary files in your Drive, and deleting the app never deletes them.

Children

Work Command is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
